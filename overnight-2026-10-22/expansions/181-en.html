Going deeper: the mistakes that make AI renovation go wrong — and how to avoid them

Most AI renovation projects do not fail because the tool was weak, but because an image stood in for measurement, structure, and budget.

Trusting the render as a survey

A generated image is a suggestion, not a record: dimensions drift, windows move, radiators vanish. Keep an evidence pack — measured plans, photos and notes — held apart from every proposal as the record of the existing home. If a render disagrees with it, the pack wins.

Removing walls with a prompt

Ask for an open-plan kitchen, and the model erases the wall holding up the floor above. AI knows nothing of load, lintels, or permits. Any structural idea in an image is a question for a qualified professional, never an instruction to a contractor.

Designing before sequencing the budget

A scheme that spends everything on finishes leaves nothing for wiring, plumbing, or insulation — invisible work you live with daily. Spend in the building's order: structure, services, comfort, appearance.

Mixing styles without a brief

Without a brief — the problem, for whom, within what limits — each image pulls the project in a new direction. Judge options against the brief, not the last pretty picture.

Losing the decision trail

Renovations outlive memories. Keep a decision record: what was chosen, why, what it replaced, what it costs. It answers the contractor's "who approved this?" and stops settled questions reopening.

Pre-flight checklist

  • Evidence pack separate from proposals
  • Brief agreed before any options
  • Three different options, same criteria
  • Structural changes professionally reviewed
  • Budget order: structure, services, comfort, finishes
  • Decision record kept current
